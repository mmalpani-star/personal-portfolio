# CAD: Quadruped, Hexapod, Air Quality Monitor and HydroSafe

CAD assembly, validation and web export for four portfolio projects.
Everything here is generated by code, so every number can be traced and changed.

| | Quadruped | Hexapod | Air Quality Monitor | HydroSafe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What already existed | The complete printed-part set of an open-source design (`3d models/quadruped/`) | 4 leg STLs + 1 servo-arm STEP (`3d models/hexapod/`) | Nothing in CAD. Breadboard prototype, parts list in the [project repo](https://github.com/mmalpani-star/AIR-QUALITY-MONITOR) | 12 STL / IGES / Parasolid files and a 163 MB whole-assembly export (`3d models/hydrosafe/`) |
| What was done here | Assembly from measured interfaces, crawl gait, validation. No new parts designed | Chassis (two plates, standoffs, hardware), leg assembly, gait | Enclosure, rear cover, retainers, mounts, layout | File identification, pose recovery, mating of the unmated parts, validation. No new parts designed |
| Source | `quadruped/features.py`, `quadruped/assembly.py` | `hexapod/rig.py`, `hexapod/chassis.py`, `hexapod/build.py` | `air-quality-monitor/aqm.py`, `air-quality-monitor/build.py` | `hydrosafe/measure.py`, `hydrosafe/assembly.py` |
| Validation | `quadruped/export/validation.json` | `hexapod/export/validation.json` | `air-quality-monitor/export/validation.json` | `hydrosafe/export/validation.json` |

The supplied files in `3d models/` are read, never written.

## Running it

```sh
uv venv --python 3.12 .cadenv
VIRTUAL_ENV=.cadenv uv pip install -r cad/requirements.txt
.cadenv/bin/python cad/quadruped/features.py
.cadenv/bin/python cad/quadruped/assembly.py
.cadenv/bin/python cad/hexapod/build.py
.cadenv/bin/python cad/air-quality-monitor/build.py
.cadenv/bin/python cad/hydrosafe/measure.py
.cadenv/bin/python cad/hydrosafe/assembly.py     # HYDROSAFE_STEP=0 skips the 189 MB STEP
sh cad/sync-web.sh            # copies the GLBs and manifests the website uses
```

Toolchain: [build123d](https://github.com/gumyr/build123d) on the OpenCascade kernel for B-rep
modelling and STEP/DXF export. trimesh with manifold3d for mesh booleans and collision tests.
SciPy for inverse kinematics. pygltflib to write the gait animation into the GLB. Blender and
FreeCAD were not available on this machine, and nothing here needs them.

Tags used below: **MEASURED** (taken from the supplied geometry or the project's own files),
**DATASHEET** (a published figure, source listed), **ASSUMED** (a provisional value to check).

## Quadruped

The supplied files (filenames end in "PCBWay Community") are the printed parts of an
open-source design that was then built. Nothing new was designed here: the work is
finding how the parts fit together from their geometry, then checking that the result moves.

### Parts and what each one is

`features.py` slices every STL along all three axes, keeps the interior loops that are true
circles, and groups them into hole axes. Every number below is from that survey
(`export/features.json`).

| File | Role | Interfaces (MEASURED) |
|---|---|---|
| `body_d` | Lower body plate | 4 × Ø5.57 bosses in a 71.0 × 71.0 mm square. These are the coxa pivots |
| `body_u` | Upper body plate, already stacked on `body_d` | 4 pairs of Ø1.59 SG90 ear holes, 28.0 mm pitch, each pair centred 5.25 mm inboard of a boss. Six Ø2.98 holes shared with `body_d` |
| `coxa_l`, `coxa_r` | Hip bracket: turns on the body boss, carries the femur servo | Ø5.97 pin hole (bottom flange) coaxial with a Ø7.48 horn hole (top flange). Ø2.0 ear holes, 28.0 pitch, on the face x = 167.86. Ø1.59 tab holes for an `s_hold` |
| `femur_1` | Box link, pinned at both ends | Two pivots **55.00 mm** apart. Each has a Ø7.48 horn hole on one arm and a Ø5.97 pin hole on the other, so the link straddles both servos |
| `tibia_l`, `tibia_r` | Lower leg; the knee servo rides inside its window | Same ear-hole and tab pattern as the coxa; spike foot |
| `s_hold` | Bearing: screws to a tab, its pin carries the femur's pin arm | Ø5.77 pin, 8.24 mm from its two Ø1.59 screw holes |

### How it goes together, and the checks that confirm it

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| `s_hold` pin lands on the femur servo's shaft line | Coaxial (pin from the tab holes, shaft from the ear holes, measured separately) |
| Femur shaft offset from the coxa ear-hole centre | 5.25 mm, the same as the body boss vs body_u ear holes: the SG90 shaft offset, measured twice |
| Femur servo spline tip to femur horn-arm face | 0.64 mm, room for the horn |
| Coxa servo spline tip vs the coxa's horn flange | z 88.08, inside the flange (87.92–89.42) |
| Exported tibia vs where the 55 mm femur puts the knee | 0.06 mm. The parts were exported as a straight leg with only the femur slid aside, which confirms the zero pose |
| `coxa_r` vs mirrored `coxa_l` | Identical (0.004 mm max) |
| `tibia_r` vs mirrored `tibia_l` | Mean 0.05 mm, max 2.2 mm: a variant with 1.6 mm pilot holes (vs 2.0) and a slightly thicker tab. Aligned on its servo ear face, it is used for the right legs |
| `femur_1`, `s_hold` | Symmetric top to bottom, so the right legs use them flipped, as the physical robot does |

Resulting leg: coxa 27.49 mm (coxa axis to femur axis), femur 55.00 mm, tibia knee-to-foot about
80 mm. The foot sits 1.9 mm off the leg plane. These match the MuJoCo model in the
[project repository](https://github.com/mmalpani-star/Quadruped-leg-kinematics), which was
measured from the same files by the same method.

### What is missing or assumed

* **No servo model was supplied.** The 12 SG90s are stand-ins (`sg90_standin.step`). The
  interface values are measured from the printed parts: 28.0 mm ear-hole pitch and 5.25 mm shaft
  offset. The housing (22.5 × 11.8 × 22.7 mm) is within the published 22.2–23.1 × 11.8–12.2 mm
  range. Ear height (15.9 mm) and spline height (26.7 mm) are ASSUMED; clones vary.
* **Horns and screws are not modelled.** Their spaces were checked: the gaps above.
* **Which corner gets the `_l` parts is not fixed by geometry.** Legs on the robot's left use
  `coxa_l` / `tibia_l` here, following the file names. The arrangement where diagonal pairs
  share parts (what the MuJoCo model used) was also checked and is equally collision-free.
  Set `QUAD_HANDEDNESS=diagonal` to build that one.
* **Mass is approximate** (236 g): printed parts as solid PLA, 9 g per servo.

### Motion

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Standing pose | Feet 125 mm from the body centre on each diagonal, 72 mm below the femur-axis plane. All four legs identical: coxa −1.5°, femur −8.0°, knee 13.2° from the exported pose |
| Collisions at stance | None. Every foot touches the ground plane; nothing else does. 45 mm under the body |
| Free range of each joint about the stance (others held, 10° steps) | Femur and knee ±90° with no collision. Coxa −40…+90° front, −80…+60° rear, limited where the leg meets the body or its own servo |
| Gait | Lateral-sequence crawl (rear-left, front-left, rear-right, front-right), one foot in the air at a time, duty factor 0.84, 40 mm per cycle, 20 mm lift |
| IK | Every foot target reached exactly (6 × 10⁻¹¹ mm) |
| Collisions over the cycle | None (every 6th of 145 frames, every pair of parts, every pair of legs, legs against the body) |
| Static stability | The centre of mass stays inside the triangle of planted feet in every frame, at least **13.7 mm** inside. The body sways 26 mm toward the supporting side, and the sway happens only while all four feet are down |
| Joint travel per cycle | Coxa 43°, femur 24°, knee 34° |

The first gait tried failed the stability check (−1.7 mm: the body was still swaying when a leg
lifted). Moving the sway into the four-foot-support gaps fixed it.

This is a gait checked in CAD with an approximate mass model, not a physics simulation. The
project's MuJoCo model is where dynamics, friction and servo torque are tested.

## Hexapod

### What the supplied parts are

Identified by slicing each STL and fitting its holes (`rig.py` records the numbers).

| File | Role | Evidence (MEASURED) |
|---|---|---|
| `j1 version 2.stl` | Coxa cradle, bolts to the chassis | 20.0 mm slot between walls (MG996R case is 19.7), ear seat at z = 29.0, base holes Ø3.0 at (±15.0, −2.61) and (0, 51.4) |
| `j2.stl` | Coxa link: driven by the coxa servo, carries the femur servo | Servo-horn pattern (Ø9 hub, 4 × Ø1.5 on an 8 mm radius) centred at (9.46, 44.5). Ear holes at 49.24 × 9.70 mm pitch |
| `j3.stl` | Femur link: driven by the femur servo, carries the tibia servo | Horn pattern at (0, 64.79). Ear holes at 48.71 × 9.70 mm pitch |
| `leg.stl` | Tibia, with the curved spike foot | Horn pattern at (−3.17, 23.02) |
| `mg996_arm_round.step` | MG996R round horn (vendor part) | Not needed for the assembly. The horn is modelled as a 20 mm disc |

That is three servos per leg (coxa, femur, tibia), matching the 18 × MG996R on the project page.

### How the leg was assembled

The hole patterns fix every joint axis. Which way each servo faces inside its cradle cannot
be read from the STLs, which leaves 64 combinations. All 64 were tested (`rig.CONFIG`), and only
one meets all three conditions:

1. No collisions between any two parts across a sweep of joint angles.
2. The tapered foot tip is the first point to touch the ground at a realistic stance,
   as on the real robot.
3. The foot lands within about 11 mm of the coxa axis plane, so the leg is not twisted sideways.

**Check on the robot:** that the femur servo's shaft points outboard and the tibia servo's
ears sit on the inner face of the j3 cradle. If the physical leg differs, change `CONFIG` and rerun.

### Chassis (designed)

A two-plate sandwich (`chassis.py`; every dimension is a parameter in `P`).

* **Top plate:** a hexagon with a flat 60 mm pad cut across each vertex. Each pad carries
  the exact j1 base hole pattern, a cable slot for that leg's three servo leads, and a
  central access hatch.
* **Bottom plate:** holds the Arduino Mega 2560 on 6 mm spacers using the official board hole pattern,
  plus two strap slots for a battery or power module.
* **Six M3 × 30 mm hex standoffs** between the two plates make a box section that resists leg bending.
* **Exports:** plates as STEP, STL and DXF (2D profile for laser cutting or CNC).

| Top plate | 362 × 344 × 4 mm | Designed |

| Plate gap | 30 mm | Designed (clears the Mega's USB-B and headers) |

| MG996R case | 40.7 × 19.7, 42.9 high to spline, 54 across ears | DATASHEET, [Core Electronics](https://core-electronics.com.au/mg996r-metal-gear-servo-11-kg-cm.html) |
| MG996R ear underside, case top, shaft offset | 26.6, 36.5, 10.2 mm | ASSUMED, measure one servo |

### Gait

A tripod gait solved with real inverse kinematics on the actual joint chain: legs
front-left, middle-right and rear-left move together, alternating with the other three.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Standing pose | Foot 140 mm out from the coxa axis, body 94 mm above the ground |
| Stride / lift | 40 mm per stance phase / 28 mm |
| IK residual | 8 × 10⁻¹¹ mm (every target is reached exactly) |
| Joint travel over one cycle | Coxa 8–20°, femur 30–38°, tibia 31–54°. All inside an MG996R's ~180° range |
| Collisions | None, checked every 6th frame of 48 (within each leg, neighbouring legs, leg against chassis) |
| Ground contact | Stance feet sit exactly on the ground plane. Nothing goes below it |
| Chassis ground clearance | 56 mm |
| Foot-to-foot span | 590 mm standing. About 680 mm with the legs extended |

The stance was moved inward from a first try at 170 mm. That try had a 12.7 mm IK miss and the
rear tibias folding into their own coxa links, which the validation caught. This is a gait
designed and checked in CAD. It has not been run on the physical robot, whose project page
says locomotion is still in development.

### Layout and why

* **Two bays** split by a full-height baffle. The display and ESP32 are on the left, the gas sensors on the right.
* **Heated sensors on top.** The MQ-131 heater is roughly 0.8 W, and the MP503 is heated as well.
  Their own heat drives a chimney flow: air comes in through the side grilles and leaves through
  the top slots above them.
* **DHT22 at the bottom, upstream of the heaters.** It sits in its own pocket, closed off from
  the heated zone by a horizontal baffle, with its own side and bottom grilles. It is 58.8 mm from
  the ESP32 and 26.6 mm below the MQ-131.
* **MH-Z19E under the display** behind a front grille, away from both heat sources.
* **ESP32 on the removable rear cover,** sliding into a two-rail cradle. A slot open to the back
  edge reaches its USB port, so the cover comes off with the cable still plugged in.
* **Maintenance:** four M3 screws into heat-set inserts hold the cover. The sensors clip in. The
  MQ-131 hangs on two standoffs from the side wall.
* **Feet** keep the bottom grille clear of the desk. Two keyholes allow wall mounting, placed so a
  screw head cannot reach the ESP32.

6. A floating clip was not attached to the body.

## HydroSafe

Twelve files were supplied. They are not twelve parts: some are duplicates, one is a whole-assembly
export, one is a scale model and two could not be used. `measure.py` identifies each one from its
geometry and recovers where every part sits in the Onshape assembly export; `assembly.py` mates the
parts, validates them and exports. No part was designed here.

### What each file is

| `Part Studio 3.stl` | Identical to `bottom face.stl` (largest vertex distance 0.0 mm) | No, duplicate |

### How the assembly was reconstructed

1. **Poses from your export.** Each individual file was registered onto its solid(s) in `lid + bottom.stl`
   (principal axes, then ICP). The lid, tank, collars and a spring fit with a point-to-surface error of
   0.00002 mm or better (mean); the discs 0.0003 mm. The 20 springs, 4 collars, 4 discs and 4 elbows are exact copies of
   one another (error 0.00003 mm), so their relative poses are exact. The tank is turned 180° about Y in the
   export; that defines "upright". The assembly frame is the export's, shifted by a constant.
2. **The export is not an assembled state.** The lid, springs and elbows are loose in it (table below), so it
   cannot be used as the assembled pose. The tank, collars and discs are close to where they belong.
3. **Mating from the parts' own features**, with every number read from the meshes:

| Part | Mated to | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Springs ×20 | Axis on a tank seat, bottom on the seat's pad (z = 26.2) | 20 seats in the tank, 20 springs in the export |
| Lid | Its 20 pockets over the tank's 20 seats; the spring tops (free length) on its pin ends | The two seat patterns coincide exactly (0.0000 mm). The lid plate's underside sits 115.87 mm above the tank's outer floor |
| Collars ×4 | Axis on a floor hole, resting on the inner floor face (z = 19.0) | 4 holes, 4 collars; export rotations kept |
| Mesh discs ×4 | Axis on a hole, top flush with the inner floor face | Three of the four discs are at that height in the export |
| Elbows ×4 | Inlet axis vertical and on a hole, inlet face against the floor underside (z = 0) | The elbow's outer diameter is 0.5 mm larger than the hole, so it butts the floor |
